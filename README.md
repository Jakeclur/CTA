# CTA: Port Solano

An original open-world driving and exploration game set in **Port Solano**, a sunny coastal city. It runs in a desktop web browser and is built with HTML, CSS, JavaScript and [Three.js](https://threejs.org/). There is no build step, no server code and no paid assets: every building, car, texture and sign is generated by code when the game loads.

All four planned stages are built: the playable city (Stage 1), missions and police (Stage 2), a living city with traffic, pedestrians, day and night and sound (Stage 3), and the expansion with more missions, more cars, shops, a garage and the docks (Stage 4).

## Play it

* **Online:** once GitHub Pages is switched on (see below), the game is at `https://<your-username>.github.io/CTA/`.
* **Locally:** see [Run it on your computer](#run-it-on-your-computer).

### Controls

| On foot | | Driving | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `W` `A` `S` `D` | Move | `W` | Accelerate |
| Mouse | Look around | `S` | Brake, then reverse |
| `Shift` | Sprint | `A` `D` | Steer |
| `Space` | Jump | `Space` | Handbrake (drift) |
| `E` | Enter a car | `E` | Get out (below 25 km/h) |
| `F` | Use shops, the garage, vending machines | `H` | Horn |
| | | `C` | Snap camera behind the car |
| | | `F` | Store the car (in the garage bay) |

After a failed mission, press `R` within 15 seconds to retry from the last checkpoint. `M` opens the city map (click to set a waypoint, right-click to clear it), `F3` shows performance stats and `Esc` pauses. The pause menu has the **Missions** list. Click the game window to capture the mouse. If your browser blocks mouse capture, you can still look around by holding the mouse button and dragging.

## Features

### The city (Stage 1)

* **Port Solano:** an 8 × 8 block grid with a glass-tower downtown, midtown offices, apartment blocks, a suburb of houses with gardens and pools, a waterfront district, a boardwalk, a beach and the sea. Landmarks include a park with a fountain, two PetroSol petrol stations, a car park, a police station, a hospital, the Solano Customs garage and the Port Docks.
* **Street detail:** lane markings, zebra crossings, stop lines, kerbs, working traffic lights at every intersection, street lights, street name signs, shop signs, billboards with original adverts, benches, bins, hydrants, trees, palms, beach umbrellas, lifeguard towers, rooftop details, and a skyline and hills beyond the city limits.
* **Collisions:** buildings, poles, trees, pumps, containers and other objects are solid for the player, cars, pedestrians and the camera.
* **Player:** a third-person character with procedural idle, walk, run and jump animations, and a camera that orbits with the mouse and pulls in instead of clipping through walls or cars.
* **Health and money:** hard crashes cost health. If health runs out you wake up at the hospital and pay a bill. Cash bundles are hidden around the city and come back after three minutes.

### Missions and police (Stage 2)

* **Missions:** a data-driven mission system (`src/missionData.js` holds the missions, `src/missions.js` runs them) with phone messages, objective text and distances, 3D markers, timers, mission intro banners and "Mission passed" and "Mission failed" screens. Missions fail if you black out or get busted, and can be abandoned or replayed from the pause menu.
* **Three missions:**
  * **First Run** starts by phone a few seconds into a new game: meet Marco at Palm Park, take his van along his route through four checkpoints and deliver it to Solano Customs ($500, plus a speed bonus).
  * **Hot Property** (after First Run, start marker outside Solano Customs): steal a Patrol Cruiser from the police station, lose the police, and deliver it to the Port Docks ($1,200).
  * **Beach Run** (after First Run, start marker outside Solano Customs): collect a medical kit from the lifeguard tower and race it to the hospital in 80 seconds ($800).
* **Wanted level, 1 to 4 stars:** crimes raise it: stealing a police car, ramming police, carjacking, reckless crashes into traffic, hitting a pedestrian, and driving away from police at one star. Police cars spawn out of view, route over the road grid, chase, and search where they last saw you. Hide out of sight long enough and the stars clear. Officers get out and chase you on foot, and if one catches you, you are **busted**: taken to the police station and fined. At 3 and 4 stars the police also set **roadblocks** of parked cruisers across the road ahead of you.
* **Navigation:** set a waypoint by clicking the full-screen map. The minimap shows a GPS route along the roads to the waypoint or the current mission objective, plus police blips.

### A living city (Stage 3)

* **Traffic:** AI cars drive on the left in their lane, take random turns, stop at red lights, queue behind other cars and people, stop for a moment after a crash and rejoin the road when pushed off it. Pull up next to a stopped car and press `E` to take it; the driver runs off.
* **Pedestrians:** people with randomised clothes walk the pavements, wait at crossings for the lights, jump out of the way of fast cars and run from trouble. A car can knock them over (they get back up).
* **Day and night:** the clock runs one game hour per real minute. The sun and moon move, the sky changes colour through sunrise and sunset, clouds drift and stars come out. At night windows light up, street lamps cast pools of light, cars switch on their lights and your car has a working headlight beam.
* **Sound:** everything is synthesised in the browser with the Web Audio API: an engine that revs through the gears, tyre squeal, crashes, footsteps, sirens, a horn, city hum and waves at the beach, and jingles for missions, cash and the wanted level.
* **Effects:** tyre smoke when cars slide and dust bursts on crashes.

### Expansion (Stage 4)

* **More vehicles:** nine types in total. New: the Metro Cab (taxi), the Outback (ute) and the Vortex V8 (muscle car). Traffic and parked cars use all of them.
* **Car dealer:** walk into the green marker at the Solano Customs showroom and press `F` to buy a car. It is delivered to the forecourt.
* **Your garage:** drive any car into the yellow bay at Solano Customs and press `F` to keep it (up to six). On foot, the bay opens your garage to take cars out or sell them. Owned cars are saved.
* **PetroSol stores:** snacks, burgers and first aid kits restore health.
* **Port Docks:** a container wharf at the east end of the beach with stacks of containers, two gantry cranes and a cargo ship.
* **Tougher police:** a fourth wanted star and roadblocks.

### Stage 2 polish and a livelier city

* **First Run, rebuilt:** after meeting Marco you drive his van through four route checkpoints (rings on the road and on the minimap) before the drop-off. Reaching a checkpoint saves your progress in the mission. If the mission fails (the van is wrecked, you black out or get busted), press `R` within 15 seconds to restart from the last checkpoint with the van put back. Passing shows the pay, the time taken and a speed bonus for beating the par time ($500, plus $150 under 2:30). Hot Property and Beach Run also have checkpoints, and Hot Property has a speed bonus too (Beach Run already races the clock).
* **Car damage:** every car has health. Crashes dent the bodywork where they hit (the panels are pushed in), the car loses power when badly damaged, smokes grey and then black, and is wrecked (no drive) at zero. The HUD shows your car's condition under the health bar. Storing a car in the Solano Customs garage repairs it.
* **Driving feel:** springy suspension that squats under acceleration, dives under braking, leans in corners and bounces over kerbs; sparks when you scrape walls or other cars; skid marks on the road from slides, handbrake turns and hard braking (they fade out after a while).
* **Car alarms:** about a third of parked cars have an alarm. Breaking in sets it off: the horn beeps and the lights flash, people nearby run off, and police within earshot give you a wanted star.
* **More parked cars:** nearly three times as many cars parked along the kerbs.
* **People with something to do:** pedestrians sit on benches, stop to take a phone call, look in shop windows, wait at corners and stop to chat when they meet someone. They drop what they are doing and run when there is trouble.
* **Wildlife:** flocks of pigeons peck around the fountain and benches and take off in a flurry when you walk or drive at them, then drift back down later. Gulls wheel over the beach.
* **Trees and palms rebuilt:** trees have bent trunks, branches and leaf clusters; palms have ringed trunks and drooping fronds. Both sway in the wind.
* **Street atmosphere:** steam drifts from some manholes (thicker at night), and lit shop windows throw warm light across the pavement after dark.
* **District sounds:** birdsong in the park and quiet streets by day and crickets at night, gulls at the beach, distant horns and the occasional far-off siren downtown, and a city hum that is louder in the centre.
* **Traffic etiquette:** drivers turning across the oncoming lanes wait at the line for a gap instead of cutting in front of other cars.

### Menus, settings and saving

* A loading screen, a main menu with the city flying past behind it at sunset, and pause, missions, map, settings and controls screens.
* **Settings:** graphics quality (low, medium, high or ultra), dynamic resolution, draw distance, field of view, mouse sensitivity, invert Y, camera auto-centre, performance stats, time of day (cycle, always day or always night), traffic and pedestrian density (off, light, normal, busy) and volume.
* **Saving:** settings, cash, health, position, the time of day, completed missions and your garage are saved in the browser's `localStorage` every 20 seconds, when you pause, when you finish a mission or buy something, and when you quit. **Continue** on the main menu picks up where you left off.

### Graphics (Phase 2)

* **Cars** are lofted from rounded cross-sections that follow each model's side profile, with real wheel arches, a separate cabin with glass and painted pillars, clear-coat metallic paint, five-spoke alloy wheels with tyres and brake discs, lamp lenses, grilles, mirrors, door handles and exhausts. Headlights throw soft light beams at night, and brake and police lights glow.
* **People** are built from smooth tapered limbs with shoulders, hands, faces and several hair styles, in male and female builds. Clothing varies between jackets over shirts, T-shirts with bare arms, trousers and shorts.
* **The player** (and mission characters) use a higher-detail model. The torso is lofted from cross-sections, with a chest, a shoulder slope and a flat back. The head is sculpted, with a brow, eye sockets, cheekbones, a nose with nostrils, lips and a chin. The eyes have irises, pupils and eyelids, the eyebrows are painted in, and some stubble is shaded on. Hair is a shell with a hairline and strands. Each hand has four jointed fingers and a thumb. The jacket has a zip, cuffs, a hem and seams, the jeans have seams and worn fronts, and the trainers have soles, laces and a heel tab. The feet roll heel to toe when walking, the hips sway and twist against the shoulders, and when standing still the player shifts their weight and looks around.
* **Buildings** have relief maps for recessed windows, frames, sills, louvred shutters, brickwork and weatherboards, real plinths, cornices and floor ledges, and vertical fins on glass towers. Walls darken with grime near the street and pick up faint rain streaks. At night each lit room shows furniture, blinds or a television's glow.
* **Shop fronts** were rebuilt: stone piers, aluminium-framed display windows with fashion racks, cafe counters, grocery shelves or electronics inside, glass doors, and only the fascia in the shop's colour. Interiors light up after dark.
* **Streets**: asphalt with aggregate grain, cracks, patch repairs and oil stains (with a normal map), worn lane paint, concrete gutters, manhole covers, storm drains, and pavement slabs with joints, chips and stains.
* **Camera finish** (Medium and up): bloom around bright lights, filmic tone mapping, a colour grade that warms at golden hour, a gentle vignette and fine film grain. High and Ultra smooth edges with multisampling; Ultra adds ambient occlusion (soft shadows in corners and under cars), which costs a lot more GPU time. Low renders without any of this.

### Performance

* **Quality presets:** Low and Medium are tuned for laptops with built-in graphics. High and Ultra raise the resolution, shadow detail, shadow range and the distance at which cars and people keep full detail, for gaming PCs.
* **Dynamic resolution** (on by default): when frames run slower than 60 fps the game lowers its render resolution, down to 60% of the preset, and raises it again once there is headroom. Turn it off in Settings to keep a fixed resolution.
* **Performance stats:** press `F3` (or tick the box in Settings) to show frames per second, frame time with the worst recent frame, draw calls, triangles and the current resolution.
* **Benchmark:** Settings on the main menu has a **Run benchmark** button. It flies a fixed 30-second route across the city at night with traffic and pedestrians, then shows average fps, the 1% low, the worst frame and draw calls. The last result is kept. It does not touch your saved game. Press `Esc` to stop it early.
* **Level of detail:** cars more than about 70 m away (on Medium) and pedestrians more than about 35 m away are drawn as simplified shapes in a few batched draw calls instead of full models. This roughly halves the draw calls in busy streets.
* Shaders for night lights, headlights and every vehicle are compiled while the game loads, so the first sunset and the first new car do not stutter.

## Known limitations

* AI drivers keep to the inner lane and do not change lanes. They give way when turning across oncoming traffic, but a driver who has waited about nine seconds goes anyway.
* Only your own car's headlight lights up the road. Other cars show glowing lamps and light beams in the air but do not light the ground.
* There are no weapons. The police can only arrest you on foot; in a car you escape them or get boxed in.
* The car physics are arcade-style and cars cannot flip. Dents are pushed into the body shape, but doors and panels do not come off. AI traffic cars take damage too but are not repaired.
* Only one checkpoint is kept per mission, and the retry restores your position, car and mission timer but not your cash or wanted level.
* Traffic, pedestrians and parked cars are not saved; the city refills around you when you load. Owned cars return to your garage when you reload.
* Pedestrians only cross the road at the corners of blocks, and a few can get briefly stuck against street furniture before they find their way around it.
* Low kerbs and steps are smoothed rather than collided with, and you cannot climb onto objects.
* Frame rates have not been measured on real hardware yet: automated tests run in a software renderer at a few frames per second. Run the benchmark on your own machine to see how each preset performs.
* Everything is still generated in code from simple shapes and canvas textures (no scanned textures or artist-made 3D models), so the look is detailed but stylised rather than photo-real.
* Ultra's ambient occlusion draws the scene a second time, so it is only meant for fast graphics cards.
* Sound starts after you click **Play** or **Continue** because browsers block audio until you interact with the page.
* Built and tested for desktop browsers with a keyboard and mouse. Touch controls are not supported.

## Run it on your computer

Browsers block JavaScript modules on pages opened straight from your files (`file://`), so you need a small local web server. Any of these works from the project folder:

```bash
# Python (installed on most computers)
python -m http.server 8000

# or Node.js
npx serve .
```

Then open <http://localhost:8000>. In VS Code, the **Live Server** extension also works: right-click `index.html` and choose **Open with Live Server**.

## Publish on GitHub Pages

1. Push the files to the `main` branch of your repository. `index.html` must be in the top-level folder.
2. On GitHub, open the repository and go to **Settings → Pages**.
3. Under **Build and deployment**, set **Source** to **Deploy from a branch**.
4. Choose the branch **main** and the folder **/ (root)**, then click **Save**.
5. Wait a minute or two, then refresh the page. GitHub shows the address of your site, usually `https://<your-username>.github.io/CTA/`.

Every later push to `main` updates the site automatically. The empty `.nojekyll` file tells GitHub Pages to serve the files exactly as they are.

### Adding a mission

Add an object to `MISSIONS` in `src/missionData.js`. The comment at the top of that file lists the step types (`message`, `npc`, `goto`, `spawnVehicle`, `enter`, `drive`, `checkpoints`, `wanted`, `loseWanted`, `timer`, `reward`). Add `checkpoint: true` to a step to save a retry point when it starts, and give the reward a `par` time in seconds and a `bonus` for beating it. Give it `requires` to unlock it after another mission and `start` to place its start marker in the city.

### Adding a vehicle

Add an entry to `VEHICLE_TYPES` in `src/vehicles.js` with its size, its shape (hood length, roof length and so on) and its handling numbers. Then use its key in a parking spot in `src/world.js`, in the `TYPES` list in `src/traffic.js` or in `DEALER_STOCK` in `src/shop.js`. The model, collisions and HUD speedometer all come from those numbers.

### Changing the city

`src/world.js` decides what goes in each block. The `SPECIAL` table places landmarks such as the park and the police station, and `districtOf()` chooses between downtown, midtown, residential, suburb and waterfront. The city uses a fixed random seed (`SEED` in `src/config.js`), so it is the same on every visit. Change the seed to get a different layout.

## Dependencies

* **Three.js r160** (MIT licence), included in `lib/`. Nothing is loaded from a CDN except the optional Google Fonts (Oswald and Inter). The game falls back to system fonts when those are blocked.
