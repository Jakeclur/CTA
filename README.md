# CTA: Port Solano

An original open-world driving and exploration game set in **Port Solano**, a sunny coastal city. It runs in a desktop web browser and is built with HTML, CSS, JavaScript and [Three.js](https://threejs.org/). There is no build step, no server code and no paid assets: every building, car, texture and sign is generated by code when the game loads.

This is the **Stage 1 prototype**: a playable foundation that later stages will add to.

## Play it

* **Online:** once GitHub Pages is switched on (see below), the game is at `https://<your-username>.github.io/CTA/`.
* **Locally:** see [Run it on your computer](#run-it-on-your-computer).

### Controls

| On foot | | Driving | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `W` `A` `S` `D` | Move | `W` | Accelerate |
| Mouse | Look around | `S` | Brake, then reverse |
| `Shift` | Sprint | `A` `D` | Steer |
| `Space` | Jump | `Space` | Handbrake (drift) |
| `E` | Enter a car | `E` | Get out (below 25 km/h) |
| `F` | Use a vending machine | `C` | Snap camera behind the car |

`M` opens the city map and `Esc` pauses. Click the game window to capture the mouse. If your browser blocks mouse capture, you can still look around by holding the mouse button and dragging.

## What works in Stage 1

* **The city:** an 8 × 8 block grid with a glass-tower downtown, midtown offices, apartment blocks, a suburb of houses with gardens and pools, a waterfront district, a boardwalk, a beach and the sea. It also has a park with a fountain, two petrol stations, a car park, a police station, a hospital and a custom car garage.
* **Street detail:** lane markings, zebra crossings, stop lines, kerbs and pavements. Working traffic lights cycle at every intersection. There are street lights, street name signs, shop signs, billboards with original adverts, benches, bins, hydrants, trees, palms, beach umbrellas, lifeguard towers and rooftop air-con units and water tanks. A distant skyline and hills sit beyond the city limits.
* **Collisions:** buildings, poles, trees, pumps, benches and other objects are solid for the player, the cars and the camera.
* **Player:** a visible third-person character with procedural idle, walk, run and jump animations, smooth acceleration and a camera that orbits with the mouse and pulls in instead of clipping through walls or cars.
* **Vehicles:** six original vehicle types, each with its own handling: the Pixie (compact), Meridian (sedan), Falcora GT (sports), Bastion (SUV), Courier (van) and Patrol Cruiser (police). Nearly 40 cars are parked around the city and every one can be driven. The physics covers momentum, grip, handbrake drifts, body roll, braking and reversing. Cars collide with buildings and push each other around. The driver's door opens and closes when you get in or out, and the game finds a safe spot beside the car when you get out.
* **HUD:** a minimap that rotates with the camera and shows landmarks, a full-screen map with street names (`M`), a health bar, cash, a speedometer, the current street and district, interaction prompts, notifications and an optional FPS counter.
* **Health and money:** hard crashes cost health. If health runs out, you wake up at the hospital and pay a bill. Cash bundles are hidden around the city and come back after three minutes. Vending machines sell drinks that restore health.
* **Menus:** a loading screen, a main menu with the city flying past behind it, and pause, settings and controls screens.
* **Settings:** graphics quality (low, medium or high), draw distance, field of view, mouse sensitivity, invert Y, camera auto-centre and the FPS counter.
* **Saving:** settings, cash, health and your position are saved in the browser's `localStorage` every 20 seconds, when you pause and when you quit. **Continue** on the main menu picks up where you left off.

## Known limitations

* No traffic, pedestrians or police chases yet (Stages 2 and 3).
* No missions yet. "First Run" is the first Stage 2 task.
* No sound yet. The audio manager is planned for Stage 3.
* Fixed afternoon lighting. The day/night cycle is planned for Stage 3.
* The car physics are arcade-style (a single rigid body with tuned grip), not a full suspension simulation. Cars cannot flip.
* Low kerbs and steps are smoothed rather than collided with, and you cannot climb onto objects.
* Anti-aliasing only changes after a page reload, because WebGL sets it when the page starts.
* Built and tested for desktop browsers with a keyboard and mouse. Touch controls are not supported.

## Run it on your computer

Browsers block JavaScript modules on pages opened straight from your files (`file://`), so you need a small local web server. Any of these works from the project folder:

```bash
# Python (installed on most computers)
python -m http.server 8000

# or Node.js
npx serve .
```

Then open <http://localhost:8000>. In VS Code, the **Live Server** extension also works: right-click `index.html` and choose **Open with Live Server**.

## Publish on GitHub Pages

1. Push the files to the `main` branch of your repository. `index.html` must be in the top-level folder.
2. On GitHub, open the repository and go to **Settings → Pages**.
3. Under **Build and deployment**, set **Source** to **Deploy from a branch**.
4. Choose the branch **main** and the folder **/ (root)**, then click **Save**.
5. Wait a minute or two, then refresh the page. GitHub shows the address of your site, usually `https://<your-username>.github.io/CTA/`.

Every later push to `main` updates the site automatically. The empty `.nojekyll` file tells GitHub Pages to serve the files exactly as they are.

## Project structure

```
index.html            Page layout: canvas, menus, HUD; loads Three.js through an import map
style.css             All menu and HUD styling
lib/
  three.module.min.js Three.js r160 (MIT licence), bundled so the game works offline
  THREE-LICENSE.txt
src/
  main.js             Entry point: renderer, game states, game loop, entering/exiting cars, health, saving
  config.js           City size, road grid, beach and boundary constants
  world.js            Procedural city: districts, blocks, buildings, roads, markings, collisions, map data
  props.js            Instanced street furniture, trees, traffic lights, signs, billboards, skyline
  sky.js              Sky dome, sun and shadows, fog, reflections and the sea
  textures.js         Canvas-generated textures: facades, shop fronts, asphalt, grass, signs, adverts
  geometry.js         GeoBuilder: merges thousands of boxes and quads into a few draw calls
  collision.js        Static collision boxes in a spatial grid; circle push-out and ray casts
  player.js           Character model, procedural animation and on-foot movement
  camera.js           Third-person camera with mouse orbit, re-centring and wall avoidance
  vehicles.js         Vehicle types, procedural car models, driving physics and car collisions
  interactables.js    Cash pickups and vending machines
  minimap.js          Rotating minimap and full-screen map
  ui.js               Menus, settings form, HUD and notifications
  input.js            Keyboard, mouse and pointer lock
  save.js             localStorage settings and progress
  utils.js            Maths helpers and a seeded random generator
assets/               Reserved for future audio and images (Stage 1 generates everything in code)
```

`police.js` and `missions.js` from the original plan arrive in Stage 2, along with the systems they hold. They were left out of Stage 1 rather than added as empty files.

### Adding a vehicle

Add an entry to `VEHICLE_TYPES` in `src/vehicles.js` with its size, its shape (hood length, roof length and so on) and its handling numbers. Then use its key in a parking spot in `src/world.js`. The model, collisions and HUD speedometer all come from those numbers.

### Changing the city

`src/world.js` decides what goes in each block. The `SPECIAL` table places landmarks such as the park and the police station, and `districtOf()` chooses between downtown, midtown, residential, suburb and waterfront. The city uses a fixed random seed (`SEED` in `src/config.js`), so it is the same on every visit. Change the seed to get a different layout.

## Dependencies

* **Three.js r160** (MIT licence), included in `lib/`. Nothing is loaded from a CDN except the optional Google Fonts (Oswald and Inter). The game falls back to system fonts when those are blocked.
